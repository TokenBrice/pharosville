# L08 — Fleet as visual objects

## Verdict
The fleet has substantially more craft machinery than the resting picture communicates: six East-Asian families, curved cloth, mon, rigging and swell already exist, yet their projection reads as scattered toy boats and a distant sail carpet. The largest lever is **authoring fleet-scale spatial hierarchy and projected visual weight**, then spending recovered attention on a few genuinely beautiful vessels—not adding detail to all 184.

## Evidence
1. **Hypothesis 4 confirmed, with qualification.** `outputs/holistic/day.png` has a dense horizontal strip of hulls/sails behind the left docks and across the far shore; the near blue USDC sail and right golden rig dominate issuer recognition. However, the foreground-to-tower inlet really is open: this is not uniformly busy water. `reduced.png` makes the near-left flotilla more legible but also demonstrates that the composition cannot depend on ships moving apart. `day-1200x640.png` compresses identities further.
2. **Hypothesis 1 partly refuted for fleet vocabulary, confirmed for visual effect.** Japanese/East-Asian boats are already implemented: nine semantic hull classes map to bezaisen, kobaya, twinhull, takasebune, junk and scow (`src/systems/garden-observatory-slice.ts:85-133`). Rigs include one large square bezaisen sail, opposed kobaya triangles, reed-cargo takasebune and battened junk (`src/three/garden-ships.ts:239-309`). Nevertheless, `selected-usdc.png` shows long pale flat cargo hulls and chunky fittings more readily than convincing hand-built maritime objects. Simply proposing “add wasen” would repeat existing intent.
3. **Clustering is not missing; its acceptance is insufficiently pictorial.** Odd anchor counts rise to seven **per band**, weights are unequal, and hull-gap-derived radii are explicit (`src/systems/garden-fleet-placement.ts:168-227`). Centers maximize neighbor distance/density, not projected separation (`:488-539`); exhausted anchorages use region scatter (`:653-681`). World-space clustering/empty-circle tests exist (`src/systems/garden-fleet-placement.test.ts:99-145`). [INFERENCE] Multiple bands' clusters, separation relaxation and long-lens foreshortening can merge into one screen-space band; captures do not establish which fallback is active.
4. **Existing LOD solves detail, not apparent density.** The nearest 16 plus leaders/attention remain heroes; other boats switch beyond 150 world units, with complementary 0.9-second dissolves (`src/three/garden-fleet-batch.ts:318-346,1730-1809`). Far boats already use low-poly family silhouettes—not full hulls (`src/three/garden-ships.ts:2248-2279`). Thus “add impostors” alone will not remove sail occupancy. The cap ladder compresses into 0.42–1.15 (`src/systems/ship-visuals.ts:161-179`); major boats can reach the same maximum, weakening leadership. Also reconcile documentation before changing it: `CONTRACTS.md:289` specifies divisor `1e7`, executable code `ship-visuals.ts:165` uses `1e6`.
5. **Sail craft already exists and is locally useful.** One 2048² atlas serves 255 identities (`src/three/garden-sail-atlas.ts:13-24`); complete marks occupy 52% of the 128-pixel cell, with contrast-selected monochrome ink (`garden-sail-texture.ts:159-208`). Cloth has belly/catenary and 6×6 tessellation (`garden-ships.ts:2857-2913`), inspection-only weave (`garden-fleet-batch.ts:247-268`) and sun transmission (`:1120-1125`). Day/rest still reads several near sails as identity banners. [INFERENCE] More thread resolution would have negligible whole-frame benefit.
6. **Motion/wakes are coherent, not proven calm by stills.** Hulls sample the water swell; apparent-wind brace/luff, motion samples and turn heel share the frame path (`renderer-ship-frame.ts:288-306,438-479,637-664`). Full/balanced tiers stamp the shared wake field; analyze suppresses unrelated local wake quads (`:365-372,499-507`). Identity sail bit zero is never furled; completely striking sails was previously reverted (`garden-ships.ts:657-686`). `overview.png` shows dense pale water strokes on the right, but their attribution needs a motion sequence. Hypothesis 3 is confirmed for fleet legibility at night: `night.png` retains near lamp dots but loses much hull/class detail. Baseline metrics report 184 visible ships, 19 fleet draws and GPU p50 2.47 ms (`outputs/holistic/day.json:48-55,103`); this is attention-limited before draw-limited.

Risk:** camera orbit, hysteresis and crowded pick targets. **Touches:** VI picture (`:8-11`), CONTRACTS `:278-300`; changing the cap-scale formula needs explicit policy approval. **Dependencies:** lever 1, camera/hit testing, DOM parity. **Verify:** preview rest/overview/selection plus camera-sweep LOD/picking tests; census must preserve eligible representation and resource caps.

Effort L. ## Do-not-do / traps
No more all-fleet roughness/weave/patina tuning first: B1's 0.075→0.050 near-hull change was rejected as indistinguishable (`agents/2026-10-02-visual-upgrade/02-execution-record.md:17-20`). Do not merely increase odd-numbered clusters, hide ships under fog, light every stern at night, restore logo plates, or furl all identities; these respectively repeat implemented rules, conceal truth, break beacon hierarchy, and repeat reverted experiments. GPU headroom is not an attention allowance.

## Invariants worth challenging
Challenge literal three/five/seven **per-band** mooring prescription (`CONTRACTS.md:268-272`): projected asymmetric massing is the goal, arithmetic is not proof. Challenge rest-frame all-hulls visibility only for explicit aggregation if levers 1–2 fail; DOM reachability remains inviolable. Keep renderer, reduced-motion completeness, colour anchors and finite budgets.
